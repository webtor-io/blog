Webtor used to play torrents and nothing else. Now it plays your own files too. The rest of this release makes the library easier to browse and the torrent page easier to read.

## My Files: Your Own Videos on Any Device

You have a video on your computer and want to watch it on your phone. Or a film your tablet refuses to play. Until now Webtor couldn't help with that. Now it can.

- **Drop your files on the home page** — the same field that takes .torrent files. A single video, a pile of files or a whole folder (there's an "Upload a folder" link right under the field). Videos, music and photos all work.
- **Open them anywhere.** Sign in to the same account on another device and your files are waiting in the library, in the new **My files** section.
- **Everything you know from torrents works.** Stream plays the video in the browser — with seeking, a choice of audio track and subtitles — even if your device can't play that format itself. Music gets Listen, photos get Preview, and Download saves any file.
- **Only you see them.** Your files have no public page and no Share button.
- **A free account is enough.** You get 10 GB for your files, and 50 GB with a subscription.
- **A dropped connection isn't a disaster.** Drop the same files or folder again and the upload carries on instead of starting over.

You can add more files later and delete the ones you no longer need.

One thing to know up front: **this is not a cloud drive.** Your files are here to be watched and downloaded, not stored. Files that nobody plays or downloads may be deleted within hours. If you want to keep something, press **Save to Vault** on its page.

Try it on the [home page](https://webtor.io/), or read how it works on the [private cloud media server](https://webtor.io/private-cloud-media-server) page.

## Genres, Cast and Directors

Films and series in Webtor now come with more than a poster and a rating.

- **On the torrent page** you see the genres, the director (or the creator, for a series) and the cast — with photos.
- **Names are in your language.** Switch the site to Russian or Chinese and the names are written that way too.
- **In the library** every card shows its genres, and you can filter the whole library by genre.
- **In Discover** catalogs can be narrowed down by genre or year.

## Webtor in Simplified Chinese

Simplified Chinese is the twelfth language of the site. The interface and the guides are translated. Pick it in the language menu.

## See What's Inside a File — and Download Without Losing the Player

- **File info.** A new button on every video and audio file. It shows the duration, the picture quality, every audio track and every subtitle inside — before you press Stream. Handy when you want to know whether a file has your language.
- **Download has its own window.** Pressing Download on a file no longer takes the player's place. What you were watching stays where it was.
- **Waiting shows real progress.** Whatever you start — Stream, Download, File info — you see the same clear steps and a progress bar instead of lines of text.
- **Listen for music.** Audio files have a Listen button, and it turns into Pause while the track plays.
- **Remove several at once.** Tick a few items in the library or in Vault and remove them in one go.

## Easier on a Phone

- **Turn your phone on its side** and the film takes the whole window. The rest of the page gets out of the way.
- **Vertical videos fit the screen** instead of running off the bottom.

## Improved Stability

- If one of our servers is busy or goes quiet, your request moves to another one by itself instead of ending in an error.
- Some videos with a less common kind of audio wouldn't start. They do now.
- If your sign-in runs out while the tab is open, Webtor renews it and brings you back to where you were — instead of showing an empty page as if you had signed out.
- Saving something in a tab that has been open for a long time no longer fails with an error.
- If subtitles fail to load, the player says so and goes back to the ones you had, instead of silently showing nothing.
- When the "are you a robot" check can't decide on its own, you get a box to tick instead of a refusal.
- The ready-made download commands for wget and curl never save an error page in place of your file.

And a reminder for site owners: as we announced last time, the embedded player no longer works through webtor.io. It lives on in [self-hosted Webtor](https://github.com/webtor-io/self-hosted).
